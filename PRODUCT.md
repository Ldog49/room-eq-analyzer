# Room EQ Analyzer — Product Context

## Users
Audio engineers, AV installers, and acoustic consultants commissioning PA systems, home cinema rooms, or studio monitors. They are technically fluent and goal-oriented: they want to measure fast, understand the result quickly, and export it directly into their DSP (typically Q-SYS). They are sitting in a dim room with speakers playing pink noise; they do not want a bright, distracting screen.

## Product Purpose
A browser-based acoustic measurement tool. The user plays pink noise through their system, records from one or more positions with their phone, and gets a spatial-average EQ correction curve with parametric band suggestions ready to copy into their DSP. The graph is the product — everything else supports it.

## Register
product

## Tone
Precise. Confident. Minimal. Like a high-end hardware RTA with a clean front panel — not like a consumer app. No exclamation marks. No friendly emoji in UI chrome. Labels are short and unambiguous.

## Anti-references
- Generic dark SaaS dashboard (blue gradients, hero metrics, gradient cards)
- Consumer audio app (waveform hero, neon greens on black, skeuomorphic knobs)
- GitHub dark clone (the current starting point — evolve past it)

## Strategic Principles
- The canvas is the hero. Every other element is support.
- Measurement data is trustworthy when the UI looks trustworthy — precision in layout signals precision in output.
- The mobile page must be frictionless: one button, no confusion, works first try.
- Export paths should be obvious after a measurement — the user's goal ends at their DSP, not in our UI.
